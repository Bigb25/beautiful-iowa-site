**This is a sample entry**, placed so the town archive has its shape before the
first town week is published. It is drawn from the Newton week prepared for
Iowa, On This Day.

Government photographer John Vachon stood somewhere above the street in April
1940 and caught the everyday row: the Western Union office, a Kodak Film sign
in an upstairs window, a shoe-shine and hat-cleaning parlor, and the Invisible
Shoe Repairing shop, with a second little sign for it down on the sidewalk.
Bicycles lean at the curb, and a boy is crossing the street mid-frame.

Eleven years earlier, on Saturday, November 2, 1929, the *Newton Daily News*
announced "Maytagers to Convene Here": about 150 Maytag salesmen, dealers and
district managers were coming to Newton on a special train, due in Monday
morning at 8:45, for two days of factory trips, a luncheon and pep meeting at
the Hotel Maytag, and a banquet to close it out. On the same front page, the
Automatic Washer company was about to take to the air — a half-hour program on
NBC's Blue Network, coast to coast.
